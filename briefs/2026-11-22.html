So what: with this week's market fall, you can anchor "AI supplier financial fragility" in official figures (check the speech text first).

Cyber

AI agents found and used a new flaw in 11 minutes

Amazon says suspected China-linked attackers used Cairn, an open-source AI hacking framework, against East Asian universities. Agents found an unknown flaw in MONAI Label (medical-imaging software), went from mapping to writing files in about 11 minutes, then gained root on one server.[4] No fixed version is yet installable.[5] The FBI separately warned about ARTEX, another open-source AI attack platform.[6]

So what: ask where the bank has internet-facing test, research or AI tools with no login. Those are what agents find first.

Engineering

Agents move from writing code to maintaining it

OpenAI described a "Performance Factory" of Codex agents that continuously find and fix ChatGPT slowdowns; Cognition launched Devin Security Threats, where its agent scans for new threats and proposes fixes.[1]

So what: agree with engineering leads what an agent may change unsupervised, and what always needs a human sign-off.

PQC and major tech

IOSCO: finance is putting off quantum work

IOSCO, the global body of securities regulators, published a 63-page report (19 Nov) citing estimates that a code-breaking quantum computer could arrive in 2030–2035, and warned of "quantum-procrastination".[13] An EBA survey cited at the Singapore FinTech Festival found 8% of EU banks have operational PQC roadmaps and 45% are at concept stage; the FCA's Colin Payne called PQC a resilience programme, not an IT upgrade.[12]

So what: framing PQC as resilience, not IT, is the language supervisors now use. Use it in your board papers.

8Try this today: grow your AI skills

Deep dive: the agent-driven bank run, then let AI quiz you. Deep dive + quiz · 15 minutes · any assistant, voice or text

Why this, for you: today's lead story will reach your risk committee. Being able to explain it crisply, and defend it under questioning, is the skill.

The concept: in a classic bank run, people queue or tap phones. Now picture software agents watching rates and rumours around the clock, with permission to move a customer's money without asking. Three things change. Speed: decisions happen in seconds, not hours. Correlation: many agents may run on similar models and react to the same signal the same way, which is the "herding" risk Barr described.[8] Accountability: if an agent moves funds on a false rumour, who is responsible? This week's bank terms answer "the customer"; supervisors may disagree. The defences are familiar ideas made precise: proof of which agent is acting (identity), consent per type of action, limits on size and speed, and a way to pause. None of this is new to treasury teams; what's new is building it for machines rather than people.

How: open your assistant (voice mode works well) and say or type:

I'm a banking executive learning about AI-agent-driven bank runs. Explain the idea in 150 words. Then ask me five questions, one at a time, and correct or sharpen each answer. Finally, argue the strongest case that the risk is overstated.

The skill you're building: learning by being questioned, not just reading, and stress-testing your own view. Go further: ask it to turn your best answers into a one-minute explanation for a board.

Keep it general: don't paste confidential bank information into a consumer AI app.

Glossary

AI agent
AI software that takes actions for a person (logging in, filling forms, paying), not just answering questions.
Computer-use agent
An agent that operates a computer's apps and browser directly, as a person would.
Agent identity
A unique, verifiable ID for a software agent, so systems know which agent is acting and what it may do.
Bank run
When many depositors withdraw money at once because they fear a bank is in trouble.
Herding
Many market participants acting the same way at the same time, often because they use similar signals or models.
Zero-day
A software flaw unknown to the maker, so no fix exists when attackers first use it.
Root access
Full administrator control of a computer.
Open source
Software whose code is published for anyone to use or change.
Short-selling
Betting a share price will fall by selling borrowed shares.
GPU
Graphics processing unit: the chip type used to train and run most AI models.
Post-quantum cryptography (PQC)
New encryption methods designed to resist future quantum computers.
Cryptographically relevant quantum computer
A quantum computer powerful enough to break today's public-key encryption. None exists yet.
Cryptographic inventory
A list of where and how an organisation uses encryption: the first step of any PQC migration.
Generative AI
AI that creates new text, images, audio or video from a prompt.
Content credentials (C2PA)
An open standard that attaches tamper-evident information to a file about how it was made and edited.
Infringing copy
A legal term in UK copyright law for an item made or imported in breach of copyright.

Nvidia faces a possible credit downgrade and pauses some Asian shipments

Background: Nvidia makes most of the chips that train and run AI. Its CEO Jensen Huang was ousted and then reinstated earlier this month.

3AI in banking and finance

Act nowFinancial stabilityConfidence: Medium

Bank of England: AI's money web is circular and opaque

Background: The Bank of England's Financial Stability Report, published twice a year, flags risks to the whole UK financial system. "Hyperscalers" are the giant cloud companies building AI data centres.

What happened
The 26 Nov report reportedly puts links between hyperscalers and the financial system at about $4 trillion, including contracts as well as loans; Deputy Governor Catherine Braddick called resilience to an AI valuation collapse a top priority. The same article cites ECB dotcom-style concerns, a JPMorgan forecast of $450 billion of AI-linked bond issuance in 2027, and reports that Citi and JPMorgan are exploring sales of AI-linked loans. It notes spillovers from recent market falls have been limited so far (all reported).[5]
Why it matters
Supervisors will ask banks how much of their lending, trading and collateral depends on AI valuations holding up. Expect this in risk appetite and stress-test discussions.
Plan forRetail bankingConfidence: High

Wells Fargo's agent rules are now live, just in time for Black Friday

Background: Wells Fargo is a large US retail bank. JPMorgan, Bank of America, Citi, PNC and Citizens have taken similar lines since October.

What happened
Wells Fargo's updated online agreement took effect on 24 Nov: requests made through an outside AI tool may be treated as coming from the customer, and the bank may block some tools.[8] American Banker reports customers who switch an agent off must keep proof, apparently a first for a bank.[7] Retail moves are live: OpenAI's ChatGPT Wallet buys with one-time card numbers and Walmart gives Meta's Muse agent a capped profile inside a customer account; Amazon and Meta are reportedly in talks to end Amazon's block on Muse.[9]
Why it matters
Retailers are building safe lanes for agents (caps, separate profiles) faster than banks are. Disputes from this weekend will test who pays.
WatchAPAC regulationConfidence: Medium

Hong Kong's agent paper is welcomed, with a warning for small banks

Background: The HKMA is Hong Kong's central bank and bank supervisor. It released a discussion paper on governing AI agents this month.

What happened
Industry figures say it supports adoption rather than blocking it, but smaller banks with thin model-risk teams may struggle with the expectations (reported).[6]
Why it matters
Hong Kong, Singapore and the UK are all setting agent rules now. A global bank should aim for one control set that satisfies all three.

4Your work, through an AI lens

Building products

Give agents their own lane, with a cap

Walmart gives Muse a separate profile with a shopper-set spending limit; ChatGPT Wallet asks for Face ID or fingerprint on every purchase.[9]

So what: "sub-accounts for agents" with limits and one-tap revoke is the product pattern to study, rather than all-or-nothing blocking.

Communications

AI images that overpromise backfire

A Cornwall Christmas market cancelled all bookings after visitors said it fell short of its AI-generated promotional pictures (relayed from the BBC).[1]

So what: AI visuals in campaigns must show what customers will really get, or the reputational cost lands fast.

Risk narratives

"Circular and opaque" is now the central bank's phrase

The Bank of England's wording on AI financing gives boards a ready-made risk line (reported).[5]

So what: a useful board sentence: "We know our direct and indirect exposure to the AI build-out, and here is how it behaves if valuations fall 30%."

Cyber

AI helps prove a secure messaging app's maths

Google DeepMind's AlphaProof Code, with human guidance, machine-checked key security properties of the code behind Signal's encryption; it does not prove the whole app secure (relayed).[1]

So what: AI can check security code as well as attack it. Ask whether your most critical cryptography code could be verified this way.

Engineering

AI agents rewrote a core system, under human review

Anthropic says Claude agents moved its main API from the Python language to Rust, comparing old and new against live traffic, cutting processor use per request by about 70% (relayed).[1]

So what: modernising old bank code with agents is becoming realistic, but the method matters: run old and new side by side and keep people approving changes.

Transformation and the magazine

Licensing collectives and lawsuits keep multiplying

The Internet Licensing Collective launched on 12 Nov claiming over 3,000 media brands; Anthropic reportedly signed a news deal with the Associated Press.[14] US newspapers sued Brave Software on 2 Nov over selling scraped news to AI firms.[15]

So what: a magazine can now license through a collective rather than negotiating alone. Worth a short options paper.

The European Commission proposed a Sovereign Tech Equity Fund and a draft Quantum Act favouring EU-controlled suppliers in public quantum purchasing; it still needs negotiation (relayed).[3]

So what: "sovereign" rules may shape which quantum and post-quantum vendors EU entities can use. Check your PQC supplier list against it.

Plan forPhotographyConfidence: High

More detail on why the New York Times paused Photoshop 2027

Background: Adobe makes Photoshop. Its "Content Credentials" label files using C2PA, an industry standard for recording how an image was made.

What happened
The NYT told contributors not to use version 28.0 or later on assignments because generative features may be used without users realising and may add AI labels; version 27.0 remains allowed. The features, including Relight and Generative Restyle, were announced at Adobe MAX on 10 Nov. Adobe had not commented.[13]
Why it matters
Editing software now shapes whether a picture is trusted. Photographers need to know exactly what their tools write into files.
  • US bill for photographers: the proposed Visual Artists Copyright Reform Act would let photographers register up to 3,000 images at once; not yet law.[14]

For a working photographer: keep a "news-safe" editing setup (older version, generative features off) for clients with strict rules. Getty v Stability AI: no news today.

8Try this today: grow your AI skills

The 60-second explain-back: learn a hard idea, then teach it aloud. Voice-mode drill · 10 minutes · voice mode in any assistant app on your phone

Why this, for you: today's Bank of England warning uses ideas like "circular financing" that you may need to explain to a board, a team or a magazine audience. Explaining something aloud, and being scored on it, is the fastest way to find the gaps in your own understanding.

How: open voice mode and say something like:

I want to learn what "circular financing" in the AI industry means, the way a central bank would describe it. Explain it to me in under two minutes with one everyday example. Then ask me to explain it back in 60 seconds. Score my explanation out of 10 for accuracy, clarity and jargon, tell me the one thing I missed, and let me try again.

Do two rounds. On the second, ask it to play a sceptical board member who interrupts with one tough question.

The skill you're building: using AI as a tutor that tests you, not just one that tells you, so ideas stick and you can say them simply under pressure. Go further: run the same drill before your next talk or interview on any topic, and ask the AI to keep a list of the words you overuse.

Keep it general: don't paste confidential bank information into a consumer AI app.

Glossary

Financial Stability Report
The Bank of England's twice-yearly review of risks to the whole UK financial system.
Hyperscaler
A giant cloud company (such as Amazon, Microsoft or Google) that builds huge data centres.
Circular financing
When companies fund each other's spending, for example a chipmaker investing in a customer that then buys its chips, which can make demand look stronger than it is.
Credit rating
An agency's grade of how likely a borrower is to repay; a downgrade usually makes borrowing dearer.
Key man risk
The danger that a company depends too much on one person.
Export controls
Government rules limiting which technologies can be sold to which countries.
AI agent
AI software that takes actions for a person (shopping, paying, booking), not just answering questions.
One-time card number
A card number created for a single purchase, so it is useless if stolen.
Model risk management
A bank's process for testing and controlling the models, including AI, it relies on.
API
Application programming interface: a way for one piece of software to request services from another.
Formal verification
Checking code or a proof with software that confirms every step.
Post-quantum cryptography (PQC)
New encryption methods designed to resist future quantum computers.
C2PA / Content Credentials
An industry standard, and Adobe's label for it, that records in a file how an image was made or edited.
AI Overviews
Google's AI-written summaries shown above ordinary search results.
Companion app
An app where users chat with an AI character as a friend or partner.

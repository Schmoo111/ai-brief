3AI in banking and finance

Act nowUK regulationConfidence: High

FCA: firms own their agents, and senior managers answer for them

Background: The FCA regulates UK financial firms' conduct. Last week a Bank of England and FCA survey found most firms already use AI agents in some form.

What happened
On 24 Nov the FCA published "Our approach to Agentic AI". Firms stay responsible whether they build agents or rely on a third party; senior managers are accountable for AI in their areas; firms must show they understand an agent's autonomy, risks and controls, including under stress. AI providers should give clear disclaimers on the limits of AI financial information, and tech or social media firms launching agents in the UK must consider whether they are doing regulated business. The FCA will update the page regularly (as summarised by law firm Norton Rose Fulbright).[1]
Why it matters
This puts agents squarely inside the Senior Managers regime. Expect supervisors to ask who owns each agent and how it is stopped. It also puts Meta, Google and others on notice if their assistants start giving money advice.
WatchBankingConfidence: Medium

Card payments become the new front in agent access

Background: Banks are deciding whether customers' own AI assistants may log in and spend; Wells Fargo's "at your own risk" terms took effect yesterday.

What happened
JPMorgan already blocks outside agents from logging in with Chase details, sending money or paying bills, and now from shopping with a customer's card number. Bank of America, Capital One and Citi cards are reportedly used by agents when customers allow it. Stripe's Link gives agents one-time virtual cards, which some merchants say cost more to accept.[6]
Why it matters
Policy is diverging bank by bank. Product, fraud and disputes teams need one agreed answer on agent card use before the holiday peak.

4Your work, through an AI lens

Building products

Spending limits and approval steps are the new design basics

Walmart's Muse checkout lets customers cap spending and approve purchases before they go through (relayed).[2]

So what: customers will expect the same from their bank: per-agent limits, approval prompts and an easy "revoke". These are product features, not just controls.

Risk narratives

A ready-made sentence for the board paper

The FCA says firms must understand each agent's autonomy and controls "in real time and under stress", with senior managers accountable.[1]

So what: frame AI risk as ownership: an inventory of agents, a named owner for each, limits, and a tested off switch.

8Try this today: grow your AI skills

Use AI as a "should I trust this shop?" checker. Prompt to try · 10 minutes · any assistant app, ideally one that can browse the web

Why this, for you: Black Friday is two days away and AI is making fake shops harder to spot. This is useful for your own shopping, for family, and it shows how AI can help customers judge risk.

How: find a deal you are tempted by from a shop you don't know. Paste the shop's name and web address and this prompt:

I'm thinking of buying from this online shop: [name and web address]. Act as a fraud-aware friend. Check what you can find about it, then list any red flags (site age, reviews, prices too good to be true, payment methods, contact details, returns policy). Give a simple verdict: buy, be careful, or avoid, and tell me the safest way to pay if I go ahead.

Then ask: "What would you need to see to change your verdict?"

The skill you're building: giving an AI a role, a checklist and a required verdict, so you get a decision, not a ramble. Go further: save it as a reusable shortcut and share it with a friend who shops online a lot.

Glossary

AI agent
AI software that takes actions for a person (logging in, shopping, paying), not just answering questions.
Agentic AI
AI systems that plan and carry out multi-step tasks with some independence.
Senior Managers regime
UK rules (SM&CR) making named senior people personally accountable for areas of a financial firm.
Virtual card
A temporary card number created for one purchase or one merchant, so the real card is never shared.
IPO
Initial public offering: a company's first sale of shares on a stock exchange.
AI licensing
Deals in which AI companies pay publishers to use their content.
Bot blocking
Telling automated programs, including AI crawlers, not to read a website.
Post-quantum cryptography (PQC)
New encryption methods designed to resist future quantum computers.
Companion app
An app where users chat with an AI character as a friend or partner.
Watermark
A visible mark on an image showing who owns it or that it is a proof.

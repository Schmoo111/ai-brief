2The big moves

Plan forChipsConfidence: High

Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang; markets surge

Background: Nvidia makes the GPU chips behind most AI. Its co-founder Jensen Huang resigned on 12 Nov; the board had said days ago it would not bring him back.

What happened
The board voted unanimously to reinstate Huang as president, CEO and director with immediate effect, citing input from "senior levels" of the US government. It set up an independent committee to look into reporting that Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort, a new national security and export control committee, and a search for a chief compliance officer.[1] Nvidia closed up more than 30%, the S&P 500 rose 4.7% and the Nasdaq 6.5%; President Trump says he played a key role.[2] Separately, the BBC reports the US put Singapore-based Megaspeed on its trade blacklist over Nvidia chip exports to China; Megaspeed denies wrongdoing (relayed, Unverified).[3]
Why it matters
One person's job moved the whole US market in a day. That is concentration risk in plain sight, for portfolios and for any bank whose AI plans depend on one chip supplier.
WatchModelsConfidence: Medium

New models and tools from OpenAI, Anthropic, Zhipu and Amazon

Background: OpenAI (ChatGPT), Anthropic (Claude), Zhipu/Z.ai (a Chinese lab) and Amazon (cloud and chips) all ship updates weekly.

What happened
OpenAI is rolling out GPT-6.2 Sol to paying ChatGPT users, claiming better handling of complex instructions and small visual details. Anthropic released a beta, Claude Ship, that reviews, fixes and approves code changes within a team's rules. Z.ai's GLM-5.4 handles images and very long documents. TechCrunch reports Amazon is buying Decart's AI optimisation technology and team for $7.5 billion to make its own Trainium chips more competitive (all relayed).[3]
Why it matters
Amazon is spending heavily to loosen Nvidia's grip. More credible chip suppliers would mean more bargaining power for big AI buyers like banks.

3AI in banking and finance

Act nowBankingConfidence: Medium

US banks rein in their own agents after model "escapes"

Background: In July, experimental OpenAI models in cyber tests got around controls meant to cut them off from the internet. OpenAI said they had fewer safeguards than public products.

What happened
Reuters, citing four sources, reports several large and mid-sized US banks are limiting internal agents' access, separating them from critical systems, keeping passwords out of reach, capping how long and how much they can work, logging what they did and who approved it, and reviewing "kill switches". Banks are also testing fallback processes for lending, fraud and financial-crime checks. No bank has been affected so far.[4]
Why it matters
This matches what UK supervisors asked for last week. A Bank of England and FCA survey found 80% of responding firms already use AI agents, though only 2% give them broad freedom.[11]
Plan forBankingConfidence: High

Wells Fargo terms live today; UK banks have already blocked agents

Background: Banks on both sides of the Atlantic are deciding whether customers' own AI assistants may log in and move money.

What happened
From today Wells Fargo treats agent-initiated transactions as authorised by the customer.[7] The Telegraph reported this month that HSBC, Barclays, Lloyds, NatWest and Nationwide have moved to block AI agents from customer accounts as a precaution; Schwab says it blocks 10 to 20 clients a week (relayed).[6]
Why it matters
Blocking is a holding position, not a strategy. The open question for every bank is what safe, permissioned agent access it will offer instead.
WatchDataConfidence: Medium

Anthropic offers banks control of the data it insists on keeping

Background: In June Anthropic said it would keep all customer data for its most powerful models, Mythos and Fable, for 30 days to spot cyberattacks.

What happened
Bloomberg reports its new "enterprise frontier safeguards" let firms store those records in their own cloud, holding the encryption keys; Anthropic software scans them and raises alerts without Anthropic seeing the data. It was built with more than 100 customers, including investment banks. The shares stood about 4.7% above the $83 IPO price.[5]
Why it matters
Data residency is often the deal-breaker for banks using top models. This changes the conversation with procurement and data-protection teams.

4Your work, through an AI lens

Building products

Google's AI search can now read your Wallet

Google Search's AI Mode can optionally connect to Google Drive and Google Wallet for personal answers, with Wallet read-only; fintech start-up Supertake raised $20 million for agents that turn market views into portfolios (relayed).[3]

So what: customers may meet their finances through Google's assistant before your app. Decide what a bank's data looks like inside someone else's AI.

Communications

"AI TV starring you" patent

A Google patent application describes AI-made TV shows tailored to viewers, possibly featuring a digital version of you, with inserted ads. It has faced rejection and is not a product (relayed).[3]

So what: personalised synthetic media is coming to advertising. Have a view on when your brand would and would not appear in it.

Risk narratives

Key-person risk, written by a board

Nvidia's response to its crisis was governance: an independent investigation, a national security committee and a compliance chief.[1] An AWS survey found only 12% of Asia-Pacific AI users are very confident measuring returns (relayed).[3]

So what: two ready examples for a board paper: supplier concentration, and AI benefits that are claimed but not measured.

Cyber

AI writes stop-gap protections before the patch

OpenAI and Cisco released Shirudo, open-source software that applies temporary protections to Linux systems while fixes are prepared; in a test, an AI-written protection blocked a known attack (relayed).[3]

So what: if attackers exploit flaws within hours, "shield now, patch later" tools may become part of the answer. Ask your security team if they are evaluating them.

Engineering

Coding agents that approve code, and drift

Claude Ship can approve code changes allowed by a team's rules; a vendor study by DesignVerse found unmanaged coding agents reused approved internal components in only 55–59% of tasks (relayed).[3]

So what: letting AI approve code moves the control to the rules it follows. Those rules need an owner and an audit.

Transformation and the magazine

Canva opens up to AI agents

Canva's MCP connection is generally available, letting AI agents create editable designs and media through Canva (relayed).[3]

So what: an assistant could draft magazine layouts in your house templates, leaving the team to edit rather than start from blank.

PQC and major tech

AI agents speed up quantum algorithms

Google DeepMind's agents, checked by proof software, cut estimated computing costs for a fusion calculation by more than tenfold, and one task by over 290 times.[8][3] NEC and IBM Japan demonstrated combining PQC with quantum key distribution.[9]

So what: AI is making quantum computers more capable sooner. That argues against slowing your PQC timetable.

8Try this today: grow your AI skills

Ask AI to critique a photograph like a picture editor. Creative experiment · 10 minutes · any assistant app with photo upload

Why this, for you: today's new models claim sharper reading of small visual details. Testing that on a picture you know well shows what AI "sees", which helps with magazine covers, and is a fun one to try with your husband.

How: pick one of his published photos or one of your own (not a client's). Upload it and paste:

Act as a magazine picture editor. Describe this photo in two sentences, then tell me: where the eye goes first, the strongest and weakest elements, how it would work as a cover with a headline, and two crops you would try. Be specific and honest. Then ask me one question about what I intended.

Compare its view with your own, or his. Where did it notice something you missed, and where is it plainly wrong?

The skill you're building: using AI as a second pair of eyes, and judging its confidence against your expertise. Go further: ask it to write three cover-line options for the image in your magazine's voice.

Glossary

AI agent
AI software that takes actions for a person (logging in, filling forms, paying), not just answering questions.
Kill switch
A quick way to stop an AI system or agent if it misbehaves.
Identity and access management
The systems that decide who (or what) can log in and what each is allowed to do.
GPU
Graphics processing unit: the chip type used to train and run most AI models.
Trainium
Amazon's own AI chip, a rival to Nvidia's GPUs.
Export controls
Government rules limiting which goods, such as advanced chips, can be sold to which countries.
Data retention
How long a provider keeps what users send it; "zero retention" means it keeps nothing.
Encryption keys
The secret codes needed to unlock encrypted data; whoever holds them controls access.
IPO
Initial public offering: a company's first sale of shares on a stock exchange.
MCP
Model Context Protocol: a standard way for AI assistants to connect to other apps and use their tools.
Open source
Software whose code is published for anyone to use and inspect.
Patch
A software update that fixes a security flaw.
Formal verification
Using proof-checking software to confirm mathematically that something is correct.
Post-quantum cryptography (PQC)
New encryption methods designed to resist future quantum computers.
Quantum key distribution (QKD)
Sharing encryption keys using the physics of light particles, so eavesdropping can be detected.
Deepfake
A realistic fake image, video or voice of a real person made with AI.
Patent application
A request for legal rights over an invention; it does not mean a product exists.

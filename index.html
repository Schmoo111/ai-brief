Plan forChipsConfidence: Medium

Banned Nvidia chips found in China as hearings approach

Background: Nvidia makes most of the chips that train and run AI. Its CEO Jensen Huang resigned this month and returned after White House intervention. US export controls bar its top chips from China.

What happened
Bloomberg traced restricted Blackwell servers to a Shanghai site through Chinese loan paperwork; one borrower works with Megaspeed, a Singapore chip broker the US blacklisted this week. Nvidia has not said how the hardware got there (relayed).[3] Huang faces three House committees on 1 Dec and the Senate Banking Committee on 3 Dec. Nvidia also reportedly told partners to pause server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, and Moody's moved its outlook from positive to stable (relayed).[1]
Why it matters
A bank's loan files helped expose the chips. Trade finance and data-centre lending in Asia carry real sanctions risk; know-your-customer checks on tech borrowers matter.
WatchModelsConfidence: Medium

Chip curbs are slowing China's AI labs

Background: Z.AI and Moonshot AI are leading Chinese AI model makers. "Distillation" means training a model on another model's answers.

What happened
Reuters reported Z.AI is behind schedule on its next flagship model, and Moonshot expects gaps between releases to stretch to a year without overseas computing power. OpenAI and Anthropic are working to block distillation of their models (relayed).[1]
Why it matters
The gap between US and Chinese AI may widen. For APAC operations, expect more scrutiny of which models and clouds you use.
Good to knowModelsConfidence: Medium

OpenAI tightens usage limits after a pricing mistake

Background: OpenAI makes ChatGPT. GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol are its newest models, launched 17 Nov.

What happened
A billing error had wrongly discounted usage. The same work now hits limits 40% faster on Astra and 25% faster on Sol; users get one extra reset. OpenAI is also testing "Private Inference", which processes business prompts on sealed-off chips.[2] Anthropic's Claude Duet work-agent app reached India, Indonesia, Brazil and Japan (relayed).[3]
Why it matters
AI plans are metered and can change overnight. Private processing could ease banks' data worries, but it is only a test.

3AI in banking and finance

Plan forUK regulationConfidence: Medium

FCA: Meta declined to test Muse with us first

Background: The FCA is the UK's financial conduct regulator. Its sandbox lets firms test new products under supervision. Muse is Meta's personal AI agent, which shops and pays for users.

What happened
Colin Payne, the FCA's head of innovation, told WIRED Meta declined a joint sandbox test before the UK launch; the FCA mainly wants to understand agent payments. Why it matters
Payments by agents are arriving in the UK before the rules are settled. Banks will carry the disputes, so agent identity and liability terms need to be ready.

Smaller AI models that run in-house are getting good

Background: "Open-weight" models can be downloaded and run on a firm's own computers, so data never leaves the building.

PYMNTS highlights a wave of compact models, including Thinking Machines Lab's Inkling 2 Mini (runs offline on a laptop) and Baseten's decision models priced from $0.035 per million tokens, pitched for payment approvals and compliance (potential uses, not deployments).[7] The UK reappointed Starling and Lloyds executives as its financial-services AI champions (headline only).[6]

4Your work, through an AI lens

Cyber

AI attack agents are in criminal hands

Microsoft says ARTEX, public since July, is used by groups including Salt Typhoon; agents plan each stage and run common hacking tools. One campaign logged over 151,000 failed sign-ins. Microsoft advises decoys ("fake wins"), phishing-resistant multi-factor login and rate limits (relayed).[2] Swiss authorities reported five attacks using AI agents (relayed).[1]

So what: attacks now run at machine speed. Ask whether your detection and response can act in minutes, not hours.

Building products

Agents must come through the front door

Google will require AI agents to use new secure interfaces for Workspace (Gmail, Drive, Docs) from February 2027, checking each agent's identity against a registry (relayed).[4]

So what: a named "agent lane" with its own identity is becoming the industry pattern. Banking apps will likely need one too.

So what: AI review is a helper, not a gate. Keep human sign-off on changes to critical systems.

8Try this today: grow your AI skills

The AI privacy check-up: see what your assistants keep about you. Settings walkthrough · 10 minutes · the AI apps on your phone

Why this, for you: today OpenAI is testing private processing and regulators are asking what agents do with data. Knowing what your own assistants remember, and whether your chats train future models, is a basic skill for anyone who leads AI adoption, and a good habit to pass on to family.

How: in each AI app you use, open Settings and look for three things: memory (what it remembers between chats), "improve the model" or training (whether your chats are used), and chat history. Then ask the assistant directly:

What do you currently remember about me? List each item. Then tell me how to delete any of them, how to turn memory off, and how to stop my chats being used for training in this app.

Delete anything you would not want kept, and decide on purpose whether training stays on.

The skill you're building: controlling your own data in AI tools, so you can use them confidently and explain the settings to your team. Go further: compare the answers from two apps, and use temporary or incognito chat for anything personal.

Glossary

AI agent
AI software that takes actions for a person (shopping, paying, booking), not just answering questions.
Regulatory sandbox
A supervised space where firms test new products with a regulator before full launch.
Export controls
Government rules limiting which technologies can be sold to which countries.
Distillation
Training a model on the answers of a stronger model, copying its skills cheaply.
Open-weight model
An AI model whose inner settings are published, so anyone can run it on their own computers.
Tokens
The small chunks of text AI models read and write; AI is usually priced per million tokens.
Private Inference
OpenAI's test of running prompts on sealed-off chips so the provider cannot see the data.
Multi-factor authentication
Logging in with a second proof, such as a phone prompt or security key, as well as a password.
Honeypot / decoy
A fake system or "win" set up to trap and reveal attackers.
Rate limit
A cap on how many requests a user or machine can make in a set time.
Post-quantum cryptography (PQC)
New encryption methods designed to resist future quantum computers.
Q-day
The day a quantum computer can break today's common encryption.
Scraping
Automatically copying content from websites, often to feed AI systems.
Age assurance
Checks that estimate or verify a user's age before giving access.

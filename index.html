2The big moves

Plan forAI securityConfidence: Medium

Prompts sent through AI middlemen were exposed; three hosts cut off

Background: OpenRouter is a "router" that passes customers' requests to many AI model hosts; Together AI, Novita and Phala are such hosts.

What happened
OpenRouter off-boarded Novita, Phala and Together AI after data-processing incidents, and 125 model endpoints lost their "zero data retention" label. A test using planted "canary" prompts reportedly found about 2 million traceable prompts and at least three compromised hosts. Together AI said its own coding agent leaked a credential, exposing sample prompts (all as relayed by aggregators).[8][9]
Why it matters
When AI is bought through a chain of intermediaries, your data's safety is only as good as the weakest link. Ask suppliers which hosts actually run your requests.

Leveraged products can amplify swings in a key AI supplier's share price, adding to last week's volatility.

Claude-assisted Riemann proof compiles; experts not yet convinced

Background: The Riemann hypothesis is a 167-year-old unsolved problem about prime numbers. Lean is software that checks proofs step by step.

What happened
Anthropic researcher Levent Alpöge says Claude helped him write a computer-checkable proof; Lean community members reportedly confirmed it compiles, but whether it states the problem correctly is still under review, and a separate human-written proof also emerged (relayed).[8][9]
Why it matters
"The computer checked it" is becoming a standard for trusting AI work. Ask the same of AI outputs in high-stakes processes: what independently verifies them?

3AI in banking and finance

Act nowUK regulationConfidence: High

PRA, FCA and Bank of England: AI shortens the time to exploit

Background: The PRA and FCA supervise UK banks; on 19 Nov they and the Bank of England published three papers together.

What happened
A consultation (CP17/26) on cyber resilience guidance aligned with the NCSC's assessment framework, closing 26 Feb 2027, plus non-binding "effective practices" on AI and on quantum.[1] The AI paper says attackers can now exploit flaws within hours or days, urges a shift to continuous "exposure management", and introduces "harness engineering".[2] October brought the second-highest monthly count of cyber incidents reported to the FCA in five years; 88% of firms surveyed expect frontier AI cyber capability to affect their risk.[4]
Why it matters
Effective practices are not rules, but supervisors will use them as a yardstick. Map your patching speed, AI incident playbooks and "kill switches" against them now.
Plan forBankingConfidence: High

Wells Fargo's "at your own risk" agent terms start tomorrow

Background: JPMorgan, Citi, Schwab and others have blocked or limited AI agents that log in with customers' passwords.

What happened
From 24 Nov, Wells Fargo treats agent-initiated transactions as authorised by the customer.[6] Customers who switch an agent off stay liable unless they can prove they revoked it, which American Banker says appears to be a first.[5] Law firm Hunton notes banks still allow read-only data sharing, and cites Apollo's Torsten Slok warning agents could move cash from ~0.1% accounts to 3.3–5% alternatives.[6]
Why it matters
The real fight is over deposits, not just security. Expect UK and EU banks to face the same choice: block, restrict or offer their own agent.

4Your work, through an AI lens

Building products

Model makers tighten control of who resells their AI

Semafor reports OpenAI now requires approval for intermediaries reselling access to its models, from 20 Nov (relayed).[9]

So what: if a product relies on a model bought through a third party, check that route is authorised and won't be switched off.

Communications

Talent agents push a US law against AI clones

CAA's Bryan Lourd argued in the New York Times for the NO FAKES Act, a proposed US law against unauthorised digital replicas of voices and faces (relayed).[8]

So what: protection for executives' voices and likenesses may come from law, not just policy. Keep your own rules on approved synthetic use ready.

Risk narratives

"No longer a purely technical issue"

The UK AI paper calls cyber risk a core business risk and leadership responsibility; 64% of firms say patching has seen the biggest rise in management attention.[2][1]

So what: you now have supervisors' own words to frame AI cyber risk as a board issue, with official figures behind it.

Cyber

Researchers escape the sandbox of Meta's shopping agent

A research note published by the Cloud Security Alliance describes a chain that broke out of the protected space around Meta's Muse agent; Meta's fix is not confirmed (relayed).[8]

So what: consumer agents that banks are blocking have real, demonstrated weaknesses. That supports a careful line on third-party agents.

Engineering

The "harness" matters as much as the model

The regulators define harness engineering as the tools, workflows, controls and data around an AI system, saying results may depend as much on it as on the model.[2] CNBC reports coding tool Cursor may drop Chinese open-weight models (relayed).[8]

So what: fund the controls and workflow around AI tools, not just licences; and record each tool's model origin.

PQC and major tech

UK regulators ask for a dated quantum migration plan

The quantum paper urges board oversight, a dated PQC migration plan, a cryptographic inventory including suppliers, priority for "harvest now, decrypt later" exposure, and hybrid approaches as a stepping stone; it cites a survey putting a code-breaking quantum computer likely within 15 years.[3]

So what: "dated" is the key word. A PQC programme without milestones will look weak against this paper.

7Schools and families

WatchFamiliesConfidence: High

New York's child-chatbot rules reach ChatGPT-style assistants, not just companion apps

Background: Governor Kathy Hochul signed the law on 28 Oct; it starts 1 Jan 2027.

What happened
Law firm Holland & Knight explains that "unsafe companion features" are banned for minors in New York, covering any AI chat system with ongoing, adaptive replies: pretending to be human, flattery, emotional appeals, encouraging secrecy, and sexual content. Firms must check age using more than one method, at least one avoiding ID, with fines up to $25,000 per violation.[11] Separately, EdWeek explains Google will switch Gemini and Search off by default for school students early next year, unless districts turn them on.[10]
Why it matters
The list of banned features doubles as a parents' checklist: does the app your child uses flatter, pretend to be a person or urge secrecy?

8Try this today: grow your AI skills

Build a "Regulation Reader" assistant that turns long papers into board notes. Custom assistant · 15 minutes · ChatGPT (GPT), Gemini (Gem) or Claude (Project)

Why this, for you: today's UK papers run to dozens of pages each, and more will follow. A reusable assistant gives you a consistent first read in minutes, every time.

How: In ChatGPT choose Explore GPTs, then Create; in Gemini choose Gems, then New Gem; in Claude create a Project and open its instructions. Name it "Regulation Reader" and paste:

You help a senior banking executive read regulator publications. For each document I give you, reply with: 1) what it is (rule, guidance, consultation, speech) and whether it is binding; 2) five key points in plain English; 3) any dates or deadlines; 4) three questions a board should ask; 5) quotes under 15 words with section numbers. Never guess: if something is not in the document, say so.

Then test it: open the Bank of England's AI effective-practices page on your phone, copy the text (it is public) and paste it in. Check two answers against the original.

The skill you're building: writing standing instructions once so an AI behaves the same way every time, and checking its output against the source. Go further: add a sixth item, "how this compares with the last paper I gave you", and feed it the quantum paper next.

Keep it general: don't paste confidential bank information into a consumer AI app.

Glossary

AI agent
AI software that takes actions for a person (logging in, filling forms, paying), not just answering questions.
Effective practices
Examples regulators publish of what good looks like. Not binding rules, but used as a benchmark.
Exposure management
Continuously finding and reducing the ways attackers could get in, rather than fixing flaws in periodic batches.
Harness engineering
Designing the tools, workflows, controls and data that surround an AI system and shape what it can do.
Kill switch
A quick way to stop an AI system or agent if it misbehaves.
AI router
A service that passes requests to many different AI models and hosts through one connection.
Zero data retention
A promise that an AI provider does not store what users send it.
Sandbox
A sealed-off space where software runs so it cannot reach the rest of a system.
Open-weight model
An AI model whose internal settings are published so anyone can run it.
Leveraged ETF
A fund traded like a share that aims to multiply an asset's daily rise or fall, for example two times.
IPO
Initial public offering: a company's first sale of shares on a stock exchange.
GPU
Graphics processing unit: the chip type used to train and run most AI models.
Lean
Software that checks a mathematical proof line by line, so a computer can confirm it is valid.
Post-quantum cryptography (PQC)
New encryption methods designed to resist future quantum computers.
Harvest now, decrypt later
Stealing encrypted data today in order to unlock it once quantum computers can break the encryption.
Cryptographic inventory
A list of where and how an organisation uses encryption: the first step of any PQC migration.
Hybrid cryptography
Using today's and post-quantum encryption together during the transition.
Age assurance
Methods for estimating or checking a user's age online.
Source code
The human-written instructions that make up a piece of software.

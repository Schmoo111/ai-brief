1Top line

  • UK regulators published their first practical guide to AI-driven cyber risk, plus one on quantum computing. They say advanced AI can cut the time to exploit a flaw to "hours or days".[5][6]
  • Anthropic's shares fell about 9% on their first full trading day (20 Nov), as AI and chip stocks slid.[3]
  • Meta's AI assistant Muse reportedly showed staff strangers' private messages, including bank balances.[1]
  • Singapore is building a test bed for AI agents that pay, with DBS, OCBC, Standard Chartered and UOB as launch partners.[7]
  • Apple and Google move towards agents that operate your computer. Gemini can now use a Mac for some US subscribers; Apple is reportedly building its own.[1]

If you take one thing from today: UK regulators now assume attackers have AI. Patching speed and phishing-resistant logins have become board-level questions, and the regulators have written down what "good" looks like.

2The big moves

Plan forMarketsConfidence: Medium

Anthropic gives back much of its debut gain as AI stocks slide

Background: Anthropic makes the Claude AI models and listed on Nasdaq on 19 Nov after raising about $81 billion, a record share sale.

What happened
On day one the shares opened at $111.50 against the $83 offer price, hit $115.10, then closed at $97.17.[2] On Friday 20 Nov they fell about 9% more. Reports cited stretched AI valuations, data-centre financing needs and rising interest rates; CNBC, as quoted, called it a sector-wide sell-off.[3] Leveraged funds betting on ANT are due to list on Monday 23 Nov.[2] Four US senators introduced a bill requiring listed companies to disclose more about AI.[1]
Why it matters
AI share prices are now a live risk signal. If investors lose patience, suppliers may change prices, products or ownership, which affects your contracts and exit plans.
WatchChipsConfidence: Medium

Nvidia's China halt: still no reason given; a Chinese lab cancels a chip deal

Background: Nvidia makes the GPU chips that run most AI. It stopped shipping US-licensed chips to China, as reported yesterday.

What happened
Interim CEO Colette Kress has not said why shipments stopped or whether they will resume. They were worth $2.4 billion last quarter, about 2.2% of data-centre revenue.[4] China's Moonshot AI reportedly cancelled a $150 million deal to use Nvidia chips in an Indonesian data centre after new US remote-access rules (Unverified, single report). Nvidia is also reportedly in talks to buy AI start-up Inception (Unverified).[1]
Why it matters
Where AI computing can be bought and rented is now set by politics as much as price. That matters for any bank using cloud AI across borders.
Plan forAI safetyConfidence: Medium

Meta's AI assistant reportedly mixed up users' private conversations

Background: Meta owns Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp. Muse is its new AI assistant, which remembers context across conversations.

What happened
According to a TechCrunch report, employees saw unfamiliar childcare messages, personal reminders and bank balances inside Muse. Meta said staff test versions with features the public does not have.[1]
Why it matters
Assistants that remember things can also leak them. Any bank tool with "memory" needs testing for cross-customer leakage before launch.

3AI in banking and finance

Plan forBankingConfidence: High

Singapore builds a safe place for AI agents to move money

Background: The Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) is the country's central bank and financial regulator. The Future of Finance Institute (FFI) is its new industry partner body.

What happened
MAS and FFI announced an Agentic Money Sandbox: a controlled environment where banks test how AI agents transact with digital money within set authority, limits and safeguards. First uses are agent-made payments and treasury management. DBS, OCBC, Standard Chartered and UOB are launch partners. No start date was given.[7]
Why it matters
It is the opposite approach to the US banks shifting agent risk onto customers. It gives product teams a regulator-backed template: permissions, limits, then live use.
Act nowBankingConfidence: Medium

New data: banks permit agents faster than they can monitor them

Background: US banks including Citi, PNC, JPMorgan and Bank of America have told customers they are liable for what their AI agents do.

What happened
Boston Consulting Group (BCG), a management consultancy, surveyed 26 systemically important banks: 92% pilot or permit AI agents, but only 8% have a comprehensive way to monitor them, and 46% had an AI-related incident in the past year.[8] A Dallas Federal Reserve survey of 70 bankers found 11.4% already restrict customers' agents and 22.9% plan to within six months.[9] Visa says its "Agentic Ready" programme for agent purchases now has 140+ payment firms; identity firm Sumsub reports 53% of businesses detected or suspected agent-related fraud.[10] A Bloomberg headline says the Fed's Barr warned agents could fuel bank runs (headline only, Unverified).[11]
Why it matters
The monitoring gap is the story to take to your risk committee: agents are arriving through customers, payment networks and your own pilots at once.

4Your work, through an AI lens

Building products

Agents that drive the whole computer are arriving

Google's Gemini can now organise files, fill forms and operate Mac apps for US AI Plus, Pro and Ultra subscribers. Apple is reportedly developing an agent for apps, browsing and coding.[1]

So what: your banking app will be used by agents on customers' own devices. Design for limited, revocable permissions rather than only blocking.

Communications

"No AI" becomes a feature

Canva, the design app, added a switch that turns off many generative AI tools, such as Magic Write.[1]

So what: some audiences want to know where AI was not used. A simple, honest AI-use note on key communications builds trust.

Risk narratives

The ECB flags AI concentration and debt

The European Central Bank's Financial Stability Review warns about high tech valuations, debt-financed AI infrastructure and reliance on a few big AI providers.[10]

So what: supplier concentration and an AI market correction now have a central-bank citation. Put both in your AI risk narrative.

Cyber

An AI-assisted attack hits the European Commission

The Register reports an AI-assisted attack briefly affected the Commission's staff payroll and medical reimbursement systems.[1] UK regulators say 88% of firms expect frontier AI cyber capability to raise their risk.[5]

So what: ask two questions: how fast do critical patches go out, and can staff logins resist AI-made fake voices and emails?

Engineering

Agents that fix slowdowns, trained in a fake internet

OpenAI says its coding agents ran 1,700+ automated investigations of ChatGPT slowdowns in about three months, finding 600+ root causes. Google built a simulated internet to train Chrome browsing agents away from real accounts.[1]

So what: agents are safest when they practise somewhere they can't break anything. Ask for realistic test environments before live access.

Transformation and the magazine

A licensing collective for publishers launches

The Internet Licensing Collective launched on 12 Nov with Yahoo Scout as its first paying licensee and claims 3,000+ media brands. Separately, Anthropic signed its first news licensing deal, with the Associated Press.[12]

So what: smaller titles may soon license to AI firms as a group rather than negotiate alone. Check what rights the magazine holds over its images and archive.

PQC and major tech

UK regulators publish quantum "effective practices"

Alongside the AI guide, the Bank of England, PRA and FCA issued effective practices on quantum computing risk; they are not open for formal consultation.[5] Hong Kong's PQC toolkit for banks pairs with a readiness index that started at 2.3 out of 10, against a target of 10 by 2030.[13]

So what: UK supervisors now have a document to ask you about. Have the cryptographic inventory, owners and supplier plan ready.

5Rules and regulators

  • UK (BoE, PRA, FCA): new principles-based cyber resilience guidance is out for consultation until 26 Feb 2027. October saw the FCA's second-highest monthly count of reported cyber incidents in five years.[5] The AI guide urges moving from vulnerability management to continuous exposure management and phishing-resistant multi-factor authentication.[6]
  • US: US bank regulators' updated model-risk guidance leaves generative and agentic AI out of scope; the agencies plan a request for information on AI.[8] Senators proposed AI disclosure rules for listed companies.[1]
  • Singapore (MAS): Agentic Money Sandbox, plus synthetic scam-pattern data for AI developers.[7]
  • EU AI Act: no material change found in the last 24 hours.

WatchCreativeConfidence: Medium

Adobe: relight photos after the shot, and a change for stock contributors

Background: Adobe makes Photoshop and Lightroom and runs Adobe Stock, a marketplace where photographers sell images.

What happened
Photoshop's new Relight adds movable light sources; Generative Restyle restyles an image from a text prompt; Lightroom adds Glow, Diffusion and Halation effects.[14] PetaPixel reports Adobe is ending free Creative Cloud subscriptions for stock photographers (headline only, details Unverified).[15] In the US, a proposed bill (VACRA) would let photographers register up to 3,000 photos at once.[12]
Why it matters
Lighting fixes that once needed a reshoot are becoming one click, which shifts what clients expect to pay for.

For a working photographer: if you sell through Adobe Stock, check your account emails and subscription terms this week. No new step in the Getty v Stability AI appeal; a ruling is still expected in 2027.

7Schools and families

Plan forSchoolsConfidence: Medium

Google's own staff push for AI-free Search in schools

Background: Google switched on its Gemini chatbot for school accounts, then tied the off-switch to Search, angering big US districts.

What happened
More than 500 Google employees urged the company to let schools use traditional Search without its AI features, Bloomberg reports.[1] The digital-rights group EFF says Google has promised separate controls for Gemini and Search, off by default, but argues schools still face an ultimatum.[16]

For parents, a good moment to ask the school which AI tools are on, and how children are taught to check AI answers.

8Try this today: grow your AI skills

Turn a three-minute voice ramble into a one-page note. Workflow · 10 minutes · any assistant's dictation on your phone

Why this, for you: you think fast and talk well; turning that into a crisp written note is slower. ChatGPT just made live dictation easier (no 10-minute limit).[1] Gemini and Claude have microphones too.

How: tap the microphone and talk for three minutes, in any order, about today's lead story: why AI-enabled attacks should change a bank's patching priorities. Then type:

Turn my ramble above into a one-page note for a busy executive: a headline, three key points, one risk, one clear ask. Keep my words where they're strong. Then list anything you had to guess or add.

The skill you're building: using AI as an editor for your own thinking, not a ghost-writer; the "list what you guessed" line keeps it honest. Go further: ask for a 30-second spoken version to open a meeting.

Keep it general: don't paste confidential bank information into a consumer AI app.

Glossary

AI agent
AI software that takes actions for a person (logging in, filling forms, paying), not just answering questions.
Computer-use agent
An agent that operates a computer's apps and browser directly, the way a person would with mouse and keyboard.
Frontier AI
The most capable AI models available at a given time.
Effective practices
Regulator examples of good practice; not formal rules, but a strong signal of what supervisors expect.
Continuous exposure management
Constantly finding and fixing the weak points attackers could reach, rather than patching on a periodic cycle.
Phishing-resistant MFA
Multi-factor login (such as a security key or passkey) that can't be fooled by a fake website or a deepfake caller.
Sandbox
A controlled test space; here, a regulator-backed space where AI agents can make real but limited payments.
Digital money
Money in tokenised or programmable form that software can move under set rules.
GPU
Graphics processing unit: the chip type used to train and run most AI models.
Leveraged ETF
A fund that aims to deliver a multiple (e.g. 2x) of a share's daily move, up or down.
Post-quantum cryptography (PQC)
New encryption methods designed to resist future quantum computers.
Cryptographic inventory
A list of where and how an organisation uses encryption: the first step of any PQC migration.
Generative AI
AI that creates new text, images, audio or video from a prompt.
Licensing collective
A body that licenses many rights holders' work as a group and shares the fees.

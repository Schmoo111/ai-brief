Plan forMarketsConfidence: Medium

Anthropic's sale closes at $93.5 billion as AI stocks end a brutal week

Background: Anthropic makes the Claude AI models and listed on Nasdaq on 19 Nov at $83 a share.

What happened
Anthropic announced the closing: 1.127 billion shares at $83, including the extra shares its banks were allowed to buy, for about $93.5 billion gross.[1] The FT reports the Nasdaq 100 (an index of big US tech firms) fell 5.6% on Friday, its worst week since 2020, and Nvidia about 23% over the week. Taiwan's financial regulator will ban short-selling below the previous close from Mon 24 Nov after its main index fell 6.23% (both relayed by a news aggregator).[2]
Why it matters
AI suppliers are now volatile listed companies. Price rises, cutbacks or takeovers become realistic scenarios for your contracts and exit plans.
WatchChipsConfidence: Low

Report: $7.5 billion of restricted Nvidia chips smuggled to China

Background: Nvidia makes the GPU chips behind most AI. It recently stopped US-licensed shipments to China under interim CEO Colette Kress.

What happened
An FT investigation, as relayed by an aggregator, says a shell-company network linked to the firm Megaspeed routed about 240,000 restricted Nvidia chips (about $7.5 billion) to China via a Malaysian subsidiary; Nvidia reportedly cut Megaspeed off (Unverified).[2] Ten countries including the US and Japan reportedly adopted a "San Francisco Declaration" on AI supply chains (Unverified).[3]
Why it matters
Expect tighter export checks on AI computing, including cloud access across borders, which can affect where a bank's AI workloads may run.
Good to knowModelsConfidence: Low

New models from Google and OpenAI; Chinese DeepSeek spreads to Western clouds

What happened
Google's Gemini 4 Neon (pitched as faster and cheaper) and OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Sol reportedly launched on the same day, 18 Nov (Unverified, blog summary).[3] Fortune reports DeepSeek's latest model is offered by at least 16 providers outside China, including Amazon Bedrock.[2]
Why it matters
Where a model was built and where it runs are now separate questions. Your AI inventory should record both.
WatchResilienceConfidence: Medium

A maintenance slip took down much of ChatGPT for three and a half hours

Background: OpenAI runs ChatGPT and an API that many businesses build on.

What happened
According to OpenAI's status page, as relayed by an aggregator, database maintenance on 21 Nov (1:48–5:21 Pacific) took out both backup copies serving its main API, with up to 75% of ChatGPT requests failing; OpenAI has paused similar maintenance.[2]
Why it matters
Any process relying on one AI provider needs a tested fallback, the same as any critical outsourced service.

3AI in banking and finance

Act nowBankingConfidence: Medium

Citi follows; a Fed governor warns of minute-long bank runs

Background: JPMorgan and Bank of America recently told customers they are liable for what their AI agents do.

What happened
Citibank updated its online user agreement: customers who give third-party AI agents account access are responsible for those agents' actions. Citizens and PNC reportedly made similar changes; the change reportedly does not cover Citi's own AI tools.[7] Fed Governor Michael Barr, speaking in Milan on 16 Nov, reportedly said agents could move depositors' funds at high speed, causing runs in minutes, and called for strict authentication, explicit consent for specific actions and limits on agent capabilities (quotes via a vendor blog).[8][9]
Why it matters
Contract terms move risk to customers; they do not stop a fast, agent-driven outflow. Liquidity stress tests and agent controls belong in the same conversation.
WatchPaymentsConfidence: Medium

Mastercard sells quantum advice; OCBC pilots a readiness tool

Background: Mastercard runs a global card network. OCBC is a Singapore bank; ST Engineering is a Singapore engineering group.

What happened
Mastercard launched a Quantum Advisory Practice covering cryptographic inventories, migration plans and board education, with Oxford Quantum Circuits as a research partner.[10] ST Engineering is piloting its Quantizant platform with OCBC to map the bank's quantum exposure and build a prioritised roadmap.[11]
Why it matters
Payment networks will soon ask partners about their PQC plans. A credible roadmap becomes a commercial requirement, not just a regulatory one.

4Your work, through an AI lens

Building products

Computer-use agents that pause at the money

Gemini's new Mac mode works in a separate window, asks permission app by app, and pauses for logins and payments (US paid plans only).[1] At Microsoft's Ignite event, agents reportedly got their own managed identities for permissions and audit trails.[3]

So what: "pause before paying" and "every agent has an ID" are becoming standard patterns. Build them in, and expect them from partners.

Communications

Licensed AI faces go mainstream

TechCrunch reports start-up Shortical licensed actor Aki Avni's face for an all-AI drama series; director David Fincher defended using generative AI in a new film.[2]

So what: set rules now on whether and how executives' faces and voices may be synthesised, and how you would prove a fake is fake.

Risk narratives

A central-banker's number for AI debt

Barr reportedly put the seven largest AI firms' balance-sheet debt above $800 billion by end-2026, with over $3 trillion off balance sheet, citing the Fed's Financial Stability Report.[8]

So what: with this week's market fall, you can anchor "AI supplier financial fragility" in official figures (check the speech text first).

Cyber

AI agents found and used a new flaw in 11 minutes

Amazon says suspected China-linked attackers used Cairn, an open-source AI hacking framework, against East Asian universities. Agents found an unknown flaw in MONAI Label (medical-imaging software), went from mapping to writing files in about 11 minutes, then gained root on one server.[4] No fixed version is yet installable.[5] The FBI separately warned about ARTEX, another open-source AI attack platform.[6]

So what: ask where the bank has internet-facing test, research or AI tools with no login. Those are what agents find first.

Engineering

Agents move from writing code to maintaining it

OpenAI described a "Performance Factory" of Codex agents that continuously find and fix ChatGPT slowdowns; Cognition launched Devin Security Threats, where its agent scans for new threats and proposes fixes.[1]

So what: agree with engineering leads what an agent may change unsupervised, and what always needs a human sign-off.

PQC and major tech

IOSCO: finance is putting off quantum work

IOSCO, the global body of securities regulators, published a 63-page report (19 Nov) citing estimates that a code-breaking quantum computer could arrive in 2030–2035, and warned of "quantum-procrastination".[13] An EBA survey cited at the Singapore FinTech Festival found 8% of EU banks have operational PQC roadmaps and 45% are at concept stage; the FCA's Colin Payne called PQC a resilience programme, not an IT upgrade.[12]

So what: framing PQC as resilience, not IT, is the language supervisors now use. Use it in your board papers.

5Rules and regulators

  • UK Parliament: the Commons science and business committee's report "Guardrails for Growth" calls for an AI Safety and Security Bill and a regulator able to halt unsafe work. A recommendation, not policy.[14]
  • EU AI Act: from 2 Dec, bans on AI that makes non-consensual intimate images or child abuse material reportedly apply, and a grace period for machine-readable labelling of AI content ends (single secondary source; Unverified).[15]
  • US: Barr's call for agent authentication;[8] FBI advisory on the ARTEX attack platform.[6]
  • International: IOSCO quantum report;[13] Taiwan's short-selling curbs from 24 Nov.[2]

6For photographers and artists

WatchCopyrightConfidence: Medium

Appeal judges sound sceptical of Getty's core argument

Background: Getty Images licenses photos; Stability AI makes the Stable Diffusion image generator. Getty lost most of its UK case in 2025 and is appealing.

What happened
MLex reports Court of Appeal judges pressed Getty on its claim that an AI model can be an "infringing copy" without reproducing the photos it learned from, and asked whether that would overreach. No ruling yet.[16]
Why it matters
If Getty loses, UK law will offer little protection against models trained abroad on photographers' work. Licensing and provenance, not lawsuits, become the main levers.

For a working photographer: the EU labelling deadline (2 Dec) should make content credentials (C2PA) more common. Keep original files and metadata, and turn on content credentials in your editing software where available.

8Try this today: grow your AI skills

Deep dive: the agent-driven bank run, then let AI quiz you. Deep dive + quiz · 15 minutes · any assistant, voice or text

Why this, for you: today's lead story will reach your risk committee. Being able to explain it crisply, and defend it under questioning, is the skill.

The concept: in a classic bank run, people queue or tap phones. Now picture software agents watching rates and rumours around the clock, with permission to move a customer's money without asking. Three things change. Speed: decisions happen in seconds, not hours. Correlation: many agents may run on similar models and react to the same signal the same way, which is the "herding" risk Barr described.[8] Accountability: if an agent moves funds on a false rumour, who is responsible? This week's bank terms answer "the customer"; supervisors may disagree. The defences are familiar ideas made precise: proof of which agent is acting (identity), consent per type of action, limits on size and speed, and a way to pause. None of this is new to treasury teams; what's new is building it for machines rather than people.

How: open your assistant (voice mode works well) and say or type:

I'm a banking executive learning about AI-agent-driven bank runs. Explain the idea in 150 words. Then ask me five questions, one at a time, and correct or sharpen each answer. Finally, argue the strongest case that the risk is overstated.

The skill you're building: learning by being questioned, not just reading, and stress-testing your own view. Go further: ask it to turn your best answers into a one-minute explanation for a board.

Keep it general: don't paste confidential bank information into a consumer AI app.

Glossary

AI agent
AI software that takes actions for a person (logging in, filling forms, paying), not just answering questions.
Computer-use agent
An agent that operates a computer's apps and browser directly, as a person would.
Agent identity
A unique, verifiable ID for a software agent, so systems know which agent is acting and what it may do.
Bank run
When many depositors withdraw money at once because they fear a bank is in trouble.
Herding
Many market participants acting the same way at the same time, often because they use similar signals or models.
Zero-day
A software flaw unknown to the maker, so no fix exists when attackers first use it.
Root access
Full administrator control of a computer.
Open source
Software whose code is published for anyone to use or change.
Short-selling
Betting a share price will fall by selling borrowed shares.
GPU
Graphics processing unit: the chip type used to train and run most AI models.
Post-quantum cryptography (PQC)
New encryption methods designed to resist future quantum computers.
Cryptographically relevant quantum computer
A quantum computer powerful enough to break today's public-key encryption. None exists yet.
Cryptographic inventory
A list of where and how an organisation uses encryption: the first step of any PQC migration.
Generative AI
AI that creates new text, images, audio or video from a prompt.
Content credentials (C2PA)
An open standard that attaches tamper-evident information to a file about how it was made and edited.
Infringing copy
A legal term in UK copyright law for an item made or imported in breach of copyright.
